A price site does not vertically integrate produce.

Vertical integration here means trucks, coolers, credit, and fruit that dies in four days. The useful product is a shared ruler so a new desk can see which layer is expensive enough to own, and which layer is a building they should not buy.

The uncomfortable parts

USDA is a lagging average

NASS prices received are national, monthly, all grades mashed. AMS terminals are yesterday’s talk. AAFC Vancouver is an asking book, not Toronto cleared trades.

The SKU is not the commodity

Greenhouse cluster tomatoes, Florida mature-greens, and California paste are three businesses. If the tool says tomatoes $1.34/kg farm and you buy vine-ripe, you will lose money.

Credit and law sit outside the chart

PACA in the US, CFIA and payment terms in Canada, FSMA 204 lot tracking. A mid-size wholesaler is looking at $50–500k just to stay legal.

Owning the middle is getting harder

The middle is consolidating. A new firm that tries to be farm + pack + truck + desk + plant copies a 1990s Dole deck the majors are no longer writing.

Nobody pays for another USDA wrapper

Harman, AgriMarketTracker, ProduceIQ already wrap AMS. The paid job is the decision: is this quote a layer I should rent, or a layer I should staff?

Where it still earns its keep

A common language

Farm, packed FOB, landed GTA, wholesale ask, retail. Forcing the basis onto every quote is the whole product on day one.

A floor the broker does not own

Public history is the shape of the year. July strawberries are always a different market than March.

A kill-switch for bad integration

If the farm-to-wholesale gap, after shrink, takes seven years to pay for a cooler, the tool should say rent.

Channel shopping, not patriotism

The fair price is the cheapest landed spec that will still pass CFIA — not the story of a single origin.

Processed vs fresh as a hard split

A juice apple and an extra-fancy Honeycrisp do not share a bench.

Who this helps

New buyer or processor

A common language for farm / FOB / landed / wholesale so the first ten invoices are not fiction.

Regional grower selling into Ontario

A public floor so a broker cannot tell you the market is 'whatever I said Tuesday.'

Food brand that wants cut or cooked veg

A split between fresh carton economics and plant economics before they sign a co-packer.

Lender or investor

Which layer actually earns the spread they were shown in the deck.

Planning ranges for a GTA buyer in 2026 CAD. Not a bid, not engineering estimates. Capex bands are order-of-magnitude so a new desk can see which layer is a building and which is a phone.

How this could actually move the industry

  1. Standardize the quote. Commodity, form, origin, pack, and basis on every number.
  2. Publish the stack, not a single market price. Farm / FOB / freight / wholesale / retail.
  3. Default to rent. Own a layer only when volume you already sell pays the capex inside a few years.
  4. Co-pack before plants. Importer before crossings. Brokered trucks before a fleet.
  5. Stay out of the daily print war. AMS and AAFC are the tape. Bushel is the interpretation layer.
