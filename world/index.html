Fresh is not a futures pit

Wheat, corn, soy, FCOJ, sugar, and (sort of) bananas have a listed world price. Iceberg, berries, and greenhouse tomatoes do not. The 'world price' is a district FOB this morning plus a boat.

Hemispheres are the inventory

When Washington apples are in storage, Chile and NZ are the fresh alternative — not a cheaper Yakima. Counter-season is a different clock and a different risk (duty, arrival, claims).

Freight is a crop

Reefer rates, Red Sea diversion, Panama, and diesel move landed GTA as much as the farm print. A cheap FOB in Chile with a tight boat is not cheap.

Climate shows up as a district, not an index

A freeze in Sinaloa or a heat spike in Salinas reprices that SKU in 72 hours. FAO's monthly food index will not tell you in time. AMS will.

Programs beat spots for volume

Dole, Driscoll, Taylor, Sysco buy on a schedule: AMS FOB ± a grid, or a kit price. Spot AAFC is what you use when you are short or entering.

Experience is shrink and spec

Two cartons with the same FOB are not the same deal if pack-out, pressure bruises, or days-to-GTA differ. Public data has no column for that. Your receiving log does.

How to use this on a quote

  1. Start with the in-season belt (source map), not the cheapest FOB on a PDF.
  2. NASS / FAOSTAT = floor. AMS / AAFC = ask. Your landed + shrink = the only number that matters.
  3. If FAO food index is ripping on oils and cereals, expect freight and packing costs up even if lettuce farm-gate is quiet.
  4. Bananas and orange juice can be checked on the pink sheet. Everything else is a district.
  5. Keep a book of your last 20 POs by origin and spec. That book beats any dashboard once you have it.
